## Part 1: CA Central Test Reverse-Engineering & Exam Pattern Analysis

### 1.1 Structural Breakdown of the Central Test

- **Total Questions:** 32–35 Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)
- **Time Allocated:** 20 minutes (average ~35–40 seconds per question)
- **Scoring:** Weighted around 1.10 marks per question (Total = 35 marks)
- **Interface:** University of Ibadan Moodle LMS (CBT)

### 1.3 Command Words & Distractor Patterns
1. **"Place the primary stress on this word..."**:
   - Format: `syl-la-BLE-syl-la-ble` with the stressed syllable written in ALL CAPS.
   - Distractors shift stress systematically: initial syllable, penultimate syllable, antepenultimate syllable, or final syllable.
   - Suffix cues are heavily tested (e.g., `-ic` pulls stress to penultimate syllable; `-tion` pulls to penultimate; `-ology` pulls to antepenultimate; `-ity` pulls to antepenultimate).
2. **"Choose the word that contains the [vowel/consonant] sound represented by /.../"**:
   - Distractors exploit spelling-to-sound mismatches: silent letters (*subtle* `/sʌtl/` vs *warmed* `/wɔːmd/`, *depot* `/ˈdepəʊ/`), letter 'g' variations (`/ɡ/`, `/dʒ/`, `/ʒ/`), and vowel digraphs (*ea* as `/iː/` vs `/e/`).
3. **"In Buchi Emecheta’s Second-Class Citizen, the statement ... foregrounds the theme of ..."**:
   - Distractors list other legitimate themes of the novel (racism, migration, poverty) to test whether the student can identify the *specific* thematic thrust of the quoted phrase.
4. **"Which of the following themes is LEAST central to..."**:
   - Negative questions designed to test holistic comprehension of the novel's scope (e.g., environmental degradation vs. social/gender themes).

## Part 2: Prescribed Drama — J.M. Synge's *Riders to the Sea*

### 2.1 Overview & Textual Context
- **Playwright:** John Millington Synge (Irish Literary Renaissance, Abbey Theatre).
- **Genre:** One-act Tragedy.
- **Setting:** A fisherman’s cottage on an island off the West of Ireland (Inishmaan, Aran Islands).
- **Core Conflict:** Humans vs. Nature (the relentless, omnipotent, and indifferent Sea).

### 2.2 Character Dossier & Fatalities
- **Maurya:** The aging, grief-stricken matriarch who has lost her father-in-law, husband, and five sons (Shawn, Sheamus, Stephen, Patch, Michael) to the sea, and finally loses her sixth and youngest surviving son, Bartley.
- **Bartley:** The headstrong last surviving son who insists on crossing to the mainland (Galway/Connemara horse fair) to sell horses despite his mother's premonitions and warnings.
- **Cathleen:** Maurya’s elder daughter (about twenty), pragmatic, assists in managing household chores and checking the clothes.
- **Nora:** The younger daughter, receives the bundle of clothes from the young priest.
- **The Young Priest:** Unseen character representing detached institutional optimism ("the Almighty God won't leave her destitute with no son living"), dismissed by Maurya who understands the primal reality of the ocean.

### 2.3 Key Plot Points Tested in Exams
1. **The Dropped Stitches & Donegal Clothes:** Nora and Cathleen inspect clothes stripped from a drowned man in Donegal; Nora counts the stitches of the flannel stocking ("four stitches I dropped after the heel") and confirms it belonged to their brother Michael.
2. **The White Boards:** Maurya has bought new white planks in Connemara to make a coffin for Michael should his body wash ashore. When Bartley dies, the planks are used for Bartley instead, but Maurya realizes they forgot to buy nails.
3. **The Ominous Departure:** Bartley takes the red mare and leads the gray pony behind. Maurya refuses to give him her blessing or the bread ("cake") she baked for him.
4. **The Supernatural Vision at the Spring Well:** Maurya goes to the well with the walking stick of Michael and the bread to intercept Bartley. She sees Bartley on the red mare, but behind him on the gray pony rides the ghost of Michael in fine clothes. Terror paralyzes her tongue; she cannot speak the blessing.
5. **The Cause of Bartley’s Death:** The gray pony knocks Bartley over the cliff into the sea; he is swept away by the breaking waves and his broken body is carried home on a plank by the islanders.
6. **Maurya’s Final Resignation (Climax/Denouement):**
   > *"They’re all gone now, and there isn’t anything more the sea can do to me... I’ll have no call now to be up crying and praying when the wind breaks from the south... No man at all can be living for ever, and we must be satisfied."*
   - Exam significance: Maurya reaches a state of tragic peace (catharsis). Her grief transforms into stoic acceptance because the sea has taken everything and can no longer threaten her.

### 2.4 Phonetic and Acoustic Devices in the Text
- **Hiberno-English Cadence:** Synge reproduces the rhythm, syntax, and vowel elongations of Irish Gaelic translated into English.
- **Keening (*Caoineadh*):** The wailing lament of the island women; serves as an auditory backdrop representing collective communal grief and mortality.
- **Sound of Natural Elements:** The constant howling wind, beating surf, and horses' hooves galloping along the rocky shore create ominous auditory tension.

## Part 3: Prescribed Prose — Buchi Emecheta's *Second-Class Citizen*

### 3.1 Overview & Textual Context
- **Author:** Buchi Emecheta (Nigerian female novelist).
- **Genre:** Semi-autobiographical, Feminist Bildungsroman / Social Realist Novel.
- **Setting:** Lagos (Nigeria) and London (United Kingdom) during the 1960s.
- **Major Themes:** Gender discrimination, patriarchal oppression, institutional racism in post-colonial Britain, the illusion of the "United Kingdom dream," maternal resilience, domestic violence, female empowerment through education and literacy.

### 3.2 Key Characters & Critical Dimensions
- **Adah Ofili:** The protagonist; ambitious, intelligent, resilient. Overcomes childhood neglect to attend Methodist Girls' High School, works as a well-paid librarian at the American Consulate in Lagos, finances her husband’s education in the UK, travels to London, endures horrific abuse, and eventually achieves literary independence.
- **Francis Obi:** Adah’s husband; deeply insecure, parasitic, patriarchal, and violent. When facing racism and academic failure in Britain, he adopts the defeatist label of "second-class citizen" and violently abuses Adah to assert control.
- **Ma (Adah’s Mother):** A product of traditional patriarchal conditioning; neglects Adah's education because she is a girl, spends family resources on Adah's younger brother Boy, and is later inherited by her late husband’s brother.
- **Pa (Adah’s Father):** Loves Adah but dies early, throwing her into dependency on extended relatives.
- **Trudy:** The negligent, dirty white daily minder in London who neglects Adah's children (Vicky and Titi); Vicky contracts meningitis as a result.
- **Bill:** The Canadian librarian colleague at Chalk Farm Library who introduces Adah to James Baldwin’s *Go Tell It on the Mountain* and encourages her writing of *The Bride Price*.
- **Mr. Noble (Pa Noble):** An elderly Nigerian immigrant who destroyed his health and dignity trying to appease racist British society, earning a house but becoming a neighborhood laughingstock.

### 3.3 Exam-Crucial Quotes & Thematic Angles
1. **Adah's Birth:**
   > *"She was a girl who arrived when everybody was looking for and expecting a boy. So, since she was such a disappointment to her parents, to her immediate family, to her tribe, nobody wrote down her date of birth."*
   - **Exam Angle:** Foregrounds deep-seated gender discrimination and preference for male children in patriarchal society.
2. **The "Sorry, No Coloureds" Sign:**
   - **Exam Angle:** Concrete symbol of institutional racism, color-bar, and housing discrimination in 1960s Britain.
3. **The Central Irony:**
   - **Exam Angle:** Education and physical relocation from Africa to Europe do not automatically eradicate patriarchal mindset. Francis becomes more oppressive, regressive, and insecure in London despite Adah’s higher earning capacity.
4. **Burning of the Manuscript:**
   - Francis burns Adah’s first novel manuscript (*The Bride Price*), sneering: *"You forget that you are a woman... and a black woman at that."* This acts as the final catalyst for Adah's departure (The Collapse & The Ditch Pull).
5. **The Courtroom Resolution:**
   - When Francis denies paternity of their children and tears up their marriage and birth certificates, Adah walks away, knowing she has sole possession of her children and freedom.

## Part 4: The 8 Selected Poems (2025/2026 Syllabus) Through the Exam Lens

### 4.1 "When a Strainer Takes in Water" (Trans. by A. Olabimtan)
- **Type/Tradition:** Yoruba Traditional Incantatory Poetry (*Ọ̀fọ̀*).
- **Core Motif:** Inevitability, pacification, natural law, divine forgiveness, neutralization of enmity.
- **Key Allusions & Cultural Symbols:**
  - *Strainer & Wicker fish-trap:* Water enters and immediately flows out; symbolizes that anger cannot dwell or be retained.
  - *Drake:* Incapable of crowing even in extreme rage.
  - *Brown monkey & Alápinni:* On the day the brown monkey encounters the Alápinni (Egungun priest/cult leader), all anger dissolves into reverent submission.
  - *Ọ̀rìjì leaf:* Traditional Yoruba herbal symbol of absolute forgiveness and pardon ("Ọ̀rìjì commands you to forgive me").
  - *Blacksmith & Iron-dross:* Slag/waste cannot be forged into weapons; malice cannot harm the speaker.
- **Sound Devices:** Parallelism, chanting rhythm, incantatory repetition, balanced binary lines.
- **Likely Exam Angles:** Cultural symbolism of Ọ̀rìjì leaf and Alápinni; identification of figure of speech (metaphor/analogy); purpose of the poem (subduing hostility and invoking reconciliation).

### 4.2 David Diop’s "Africa"
- **Movement:** Négritude Movement (Anti-colonialist, Afro-affirmative).
- **Structure:** Free verse apostrophe with dynamic conversational shift.
- **Key Imagery & Devices:**
  - *Apostrophe:* Direct address to the continent: *"Africa my Africa / Africa of proud warriors..."*
  - *Metaphor of Blood and Sweat:* *"Your black blood spilt over the fields / The blood of your sweat / The sweat of your toil..."* (Anadiplosis / stepped repetition).
  - *The Sjambok:* The heavy colonial whip that stripes the back red.
  - *The Young and Sturdy Tree:* Metaphor for the resurrected Africa growing patiently alone amid "white and faded flowers" (the dying colonial empires).
  - *The Bitter Taste of Liberty:* Oxymoron/Paradox reflecting the bloody, arduous cost of independence.
- **Phonetics & Rhythm:** Staccato rhythmic cadences echoing drumbeats; plosive alliteration (`b`, `p`, `t`) reinforcing violence.
- **Likely Exam Angles:** Meaning of "young and sturdy tree"; identification of apostrophe and anadiplosis; historical context of slavery and colonial oppression.

### 4.3 Letlhogonolo Swaratlhe’s "I am a Feminist, Not Mere Ash"
- **Thematic Core:** Radical Black African Feminism, bodily autonomy, rejection of patriarchal domestic subjugation.
- **Key Lines & Biblical Allusion:**
  - *"Some households ask that you leave your shoes at the front door before you enter, / My friend’s cousin asked that I do the same with my feminism."*
  - Myth rejected: *"that women fell out of their mothers’ wombs to exist as mere ash, / And men the relentless raging fires."*
  - Biblical citation: *"Luke 23:34: Father, forgive them for they do not know what they are doing."*
  - Metaphor: *"they have made warm homes out of prisons... it is the invisible shackles that are the deadliest."*
- **Rhetorical Style:** Conversational dialogue, irony, intertextuality, defiance against verbal insults ("Bitch").
- **Likely Exam Angles:** Significance of Luke 23:34; metaphor of "invisible shackles"; antithesis between "ash" and "raging fire."

### 4.4 William Wordsworth’s "My Heart Leaps Up"
- **Movement:** English Romanticism.
- **Famous Line:** *"The Child is father of the Man;"* (Classic **Paradox** tested in CA Question 19).
- **Core Themes:** Childhood innocence, continuity of human nature, pantheistic connection with nature, "natural piety."
- **Key Poetic Devices:**
  - *Paradox:* The child's natural wonder shapes the moral foundation of the adult.
  - *Epigram:* Compact philosophical truth.
  - *Anaphora:* *"So was it when my life began; / So is it now I am a man; / So be it when I shall grow old..."*
- **Exam Weight:** High likelihood of testing paradox, theme of natural piety, and Romantic celebration of childhood.

### 4.5 William Butler Yeats’s "The Second Coming"
- **Context:** Post-World War I disillusionment, Irish War of Independence (1919–1920).
- **Key Lines & Symbolic Constructs:**
  - *"Turning and turning in the widening gyre / The falcon cannot hear the falconer;"* (Symbol of cyclical collapse and lost spiritual control).
  - *"Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold; / Mere anarchy is loosed upon the world,"* (Direct origin of Chinua Achebe's novel title).
  - *"The best lack all conviction, while the worst / Are full of passionate intensity."* (Moral crisis of modern civilization).
  - *Spiritus Mundi & The Rough Beast:* Sphinx-like creature with lion body and man’s head crouching in desert sands, slouching towards Bethlehem to replace the Christian era with brutal totalitarianism.
- **Sound Devices:** Trochaic disruptions, ominous heavy stresses, assonance (`widening gyre`, `blood-dimmed tide`).
- **Likely Exam Angles:** Meaning of the "widening gyre"; allusions to *Spiritus Mundi*; identifying antithesis in "best lack all conviction / worst are full of passionate intensity."

### 4.6 R.E.G. Armattoe’s "Servant-Kings"
- **Context:** Ghanaian poet/physician critiquing sycophantic, puppet African rulers who collude with colonial powers against their own citizens.
- **Key Lines:**
  - *"Leave them alone, / Leave them to be / Men lost to shame, / To honour lost! / Servant kinglets, / Riding to war / Against their own..."*
- **Devices:**
  - *Oxymoron:* "Servant-kings" (a king cannot be a servant; highlights puppet status).
  - *Diminutive Derision:* "kinglets" (diminutive suffix `-let` mocking their petty power).
  - *Chiasmus / Inversion:* *"Men lost to shame, / To honour lost!"*
  - *Refrain / Repetition:* Emphasizes bitter abandonment of compromised leaders to history's judgment.
- **Likely Exam Angles:** Irony and oxymoron in the title; political satire targeting comprador elites.

### 4.7 Tayo Olafioye’s "The Conclave of Hyenas"
- **Context:** Satirical indictment of Nigerian military lootocracy and bureaucratic corruption under General Sani Abacha.
- **Key Allegorical Figures & Footnotes:**
  - *Elephant:* Nigerian national treasury and commonwealth being plundered.
  - *GS:* Government Secretary / General Staff.
  - *Lagido:* Money / cash loot.
  - *Ikoko:* The wolf; *Ikeregbe:* The goat; *Ogidan:* The leopard (General Abacha).
  - *Contract scam:* *"Festival of smut from above"*, bogus shell companies (*Cee Chris, Centre & Point, Asso Logistics*), signing contracts with their toes, laptops by Tritech Computers.
  - *Voltore:* Allusion to Ben Jonson's *Volpone* (Voltore = the vulture, the greedy sycophant).
  - *"Niger stones shed tears and blood"* (Personification of national suffering).
- **Sound Devices:** Harsh guttural consonants, animalistic imagery, satirical mock-heroic tone.
- **Likely Exam Angles:** Meaning of the allegorical "elephant"; identification of Abacha's regime; meaning of "Voltore."

### 4.8 Claude McKay’s "If We Must Die"
- **Context:** Harlem Renaissance; 1919 "Red Summer" race riots in the United States.
- **Form:** Shakespearean (English) Sonnet: 14 lines, iambic pentameter, rhyme scheme `ABAB CDCD EFEF GG`.
- **Key Metaphors & Antitheses:**
  - *Hogs vs. Noble Men:* Rejecting death like penned hogs hunted by "mad and hungry dogs."
  - *Monsters & Cowardly Pack:* Describing the racist mob.
  - *Heroic Defiance:* *"Like men we'll face the murderous, cowardly pack, / Pressed to the wall, dying, but fighting back!"*
- **Rhythm & Meter:** Robust iambic pentameter with metrical substitutions for martial urgency.
- **Likely Exam Angles:** Identifying the poem as a Shakespearean sonnet; contrast between "hogs" and "noble men"; Harlem Renaissance civil rights themes.

## Part 5: Phonetics & Phonology Exam Focus (Dr. Adenike Akinjobi)

### 5.1 Organs of Speech & Airstream Mechanisms
- **Airstream Mechanism:** English speech sounds exclusively utilize the **pulmonic egressive** airstream mechanism (air pushed out from the lungs through the oral/nasal tract).
- **Organs of Speech:**
  - *Larynx & Vocal Cords:* Houses the glottis. Rapid vibration produces **voiced** sounds; open cords produce **voiceless** sounds.
  - *Pharynx:* Tube connecting larynx to oral and nasal cavities.
  - *Soft Palate (Velum):* Raised for oral sounds; lowered for nasal sounds (`/m/`, `/n/`, `/ŋ/`).
  - *Hard Palate:* Bony roof of mouth (palatal sounds like `/j/`).
  - *Alveolar Ridge (Teeth Ridge):* Important for `/t/`, `/d/`, `/s/`, `/z/`, `/n/`, `/l/`.
  - *Teeth:* Dental sounds (`/θ/`, `/ð/`).
  - *Lips:* Bilabials (`/p/`, `/b/`, `/m/`, `/w/`) and Labiodentals (`/f/`, `/v/`).

### 5.2 Consonant Classification Matrix
Every consonant in English is classified by three parameters:
1. **Voicing:** Voiced or Voiceless.
2. **Place of Articulation:** Bilabial, Labiodental, Dental, Alveolar, Post-alveolar, Palato-alveolar, Palatal, Velar, Glottal.
3. **Manner of Articulation:** Plosive (Stop), Fricative, Affricate, Nasal, Lateral, Approximant (Semi-vowel).

| Phoneme | Description | High-Frequency Exam Examples | Tricky Silent / Distractor Words |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `/t/` | Voiceless alveolar plosive | *tea, water, jumped, subtle* | *subtle* (silent *b*), *debt* (silent *b*), *listen* (silent *t*), *castle* (silent *t*) |
| `/d/` | Voiced alveolar plosive | *day, ladder, played, begged* | *handsome* (silent *d*), *Wednesday* (silent *d*) |
| `/k/` | Voiceless velar plosive | *cat, kitchen, ache, choir, antique* | *knife* (silent *k*), *knight* (silent *k*), *know* (silent *k*) |
| `/ɡ/` | Voiced velar plosive | *go, dagger, ghost, plague, league* | *gnat* (silent *g*), *phlegm* (silent *g*), *sign* (silent *g*) |
| `/θ/` | Voiceless dental fricative | *thin, thought, breath, ether, bath* | Distinguish from `/ð/` (*then, bathe, feather*) |
| `/ð/` | Voiced dental fricative | *this, father, breathe, smooth, though* | Distinguish from `/d/` and `/θ/` |
| `/ʃ/` | Voiceless palato-alveolar fricative | *shoe, tension, mission, champagne, chef* | *sugar* (starts with `/ʃ/`), *sure* (starts with `/ʃ/`) |
| `/ʒ/` | Voiced palato-alveolar fricative | *measure, vision, beige, mirage, garage* | High-yield CA question! Often spelled *s* or *ge* |
| `/tʃ/` | Voiceless palato-alveolar affricate | *chair, nature, batch, picture, church* | *question* (`/kwestʃən/`) |
| `/dʒ/` | Voiced palato-alveolar affricate | *jam, giant, judge, soldier, education* | *gigantic* (first *g* is `/dʒ/`, second is `/ɡ/`) |
| `/ŋ/` | Voiced velar nasal | *sing, ring, sink, think, anxious* | *singer* (`/sɪŋə/` - no `/ɡ/`), *finger* (`/fɪŋɡə/` - with `/ɡ/`) |

### 5.3 Inflectional Morphology Sound Rules
- **The Past Tense `-ed` Morpheme:**
  1. Realized as `/t/` after voiceless consonants (except `/t/`): *asked*, *laughed*, *jumped*, *passed*, *psyched* (CA Question 8!).
  2. Realized as `/d/` after voiced consonants and vowels: *bagged*, *loved*, *robbed*, *played*, *warmed*.
  3. Realized as `/ɪd/` after alveolar stops `/t/` and `/d/`: *wanted*, *needed*, *started*.
- **The Plural / 3rd Person Singular `-s/-es` Morpheme:**
  1. Realized as `/s/` after voiceless consonants: *cats*, *books*, *laughs*.
  2. Realized as `/z/` after voiced consonants and vowels: *dogs*, *boys*, *days*, *pens*.
  3. Realized as `/ɪz/` after sibilants (`/s, z, ʃ, ʒ, tʃ, dʒ/`): *busses*, *watches*, *judges*, *boxes*.

### 5.4 Vowel Systems (Monophthongs & Diphthongs)
- **Front Vowels:** `/iː/` (*seat, machine*), `/ɪ/` (*sit*), `/e/` (*settler, bread, said*), `/æ/` (*cat*).
- **Central Vowels:** `/ʌ/` (*cup, blood, love*), `/ɜː/` (*bird, girl, heard*), `/ə/` (*schwa: ago, teacher, doctor*).
- **Back Vowels:** `/uː/` (*boot, shoe, through*), `/ʊ/` (*put, book, could*), `/ɔː/` (*caught, cord, thought*), `/ɒ/` (*pot, what*), `/ɑː/` (*car, father, class*).
- **Diphthongs:**
  - Closing to `/ɪ/`: `/eɪ/` (*say*), `/aɪ/` (*my*), `/ɔɪ/` (*boy*).
  - Closing to `/ʊ/`: `/əʊ/` (*go, home*), `/aʊ/` (*now, cow*).
  - Centring to `/ə/`: `/ɪə/` (*ear, here*), `/eə/` (*air, care*), `/ʊə/` (*poor, tour*).

## Part 6: Stress, Rhythm & Intonation Exam Focus (Dr. Adenike Akinjobi)

### 6.1 Suffix Stress Rules
1. **Stress on Penultimate Syllable (Second to Last):**
   - `-ic` / `-ical`: *demoCRAtic, ecoNOmic, draMAtic, terRIfic, sympaTHEtic*
   - `-ion` / `-tion` / `-sion`: *falsifiCAtion, eduCAtion, proPOSion, teleVIsion, comPLEtion*
   - `-ial` / `-ian`: *coloNIal, musiCIan, ciVIliand, comMERcial*
   - `-eous` / `-ious`: *couRAgeous, outRAgeous, lasCIvious, inFEctious*
2. **Stress on Antepenultimate Syllable (Third to Last):**
   - `-ity`: *inferiORity, caPAcity, natioNAlity, eQUAlity, cuRIOsity*
   - `-ology`: *audiOLogy, biOLogy, socioLOgy, terminoLOgy*
   - `-graphy`: *geOGraphy, phoTOgraphy, bibliOGraphy*
   - `-ate` (tri-syllabic verbs): *comPLIcate, eDUcate, inVEStigate*
3. **Stress on the Suffix Itself (Stress-Attracting Suffixes):**
   - `-ee`: *employEE, refuGEE, traiNEE*
   - `-eer`: *engiNEER, volunTEER*
   - `-esque`: *pictuRESQUE*
   - `-ette`: *cigaRETTE*
4. **Stress Neutral Suffixes (Do Not Change Root Word Stress):**
   - `-ful`, `-less`, `-ly`, `-ness`, `-ment`, `-able`, `-ish`, `-ing`, `-ed`, `-er`: *WOn-der -> WOn-der-ful; e-STA-blish -> e-STA-blish-ment; pro-FES-sion-al -> un-pro-FES-sion-al*.

### 6.2 Phrasal Verbs vs. Compound Nouns
| Lexical Item | Phrasal Verb (Stress 2nd Element) | Compound Noun (Stress 1st Element) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| let down | They will let **DOWN** the ropes | That was a terrible **LET**down |
| print out | Please print **OUT** the document | I need a clean **PRINT**out |
| turn off | You must turn **OFF** the engine | Take the second **TURN**off |
| set up | We should set **UP** the tent | The software **SET**up is quick |
| take over | The army will take **OVER** power | The hostile **TAKE**over succeeded |
| pass by | The cars pass **BY** daily | An innocent **PASS**erby |

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| convert | He is a new **CON**vert | We must con**VERT** the file |
| export | Crude oil is our chief **EX**port | We ex**PORT** raw rubber |
| import | Rice is a costly **IM**port | They im**PORT** technology |

| conduct | Her **CON**duct is admirable | He will con**DUCT** the choir |
| suspect | The chief **SUS**pect fled | I sus**PECT** foul play |

### 6.4 Sentence Stress & Nuclear Stress
- **Open / Lexical Classes (Stressed):** Nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs.
- **Closed / Grammatical Classes (Unstressed):** Prepositions, pronouns, articles, auxiliary verbs, conjunctions.
- **Nuclear Stress (Tonic Syllable):** The *last* stressed lexical syllable in an intonation unit receives the greatest prominence and the pitch movement.
  - Example: *Em'manuel is a very handsome 'man.* -> **man** is the tonic syllable.

### 6.5 Intonation Tunes & Pragmatic Functions
1. **Falling Tune (`↘`):** Indicates finality, completeness, certainty.
   - Declarative statements: *She works at the hospital ↘*
   - Wh-Questions: *What is your father’s name? ↘*
   - Direct Commands: *Shut that door immediately ↘*
   - Exclamations: *What a brilliant performance! ↘*
2. **Rising Tune (`↗`):** Indicates non-finality, inquiry, tentativeness, politeness.
   - Polar (Yes/No) Questions: *Are you coming to class today? ↗*
   - Declaratives spoken as questions: *You bought the car? ↗*
   - Polite Requests: *Could you please pass the salt? ↗*
   - Listing items (non-final elements): *I bought apples ↗, oranges ↗, and bananas ↘*.
3. **Fall-Rise Tune (`↘↗`):** Indicates reservation, doubt, contrast, conditional meaning, or polite warning.
   - *I like the color (but the price is too high) ↘↗*.

## Part 7: Literary Appreciation & Writing Skills (Prof. Ayo Kehinde & Dr. O. Egbokhare)

### 7.1 Key Figures of Speech in Exam Context
- **Synecdoche:** Part for whole or whole for part (*Twenty sails entered the harbour*; *ABCs for alphabet*).
- **Metonymy:** Substituting an associated attribute for the entity (*The crown ordered an inquiry*; *The pen is mightier than the sword*).
- **Paradox:** A seemingly self-contradictory statement that reveals a profound underlying truth (*The Child is father of the Man*).
- **Oxymoron:** Two contradictory terms placed side-by-side (*wise fool, pretty ugly, darkness visible*).
- **Litotes:** Deliberate understatement by negating the opposite (*He is no fool; She was not unmindful*).
- **Apostrophe:** Addressing an absent person, deity, or personified object (*Death, be not proud; Busy old fool, unruly Sun*).
- **Chiasmus:** Reversal of grammatical structures in successive phrases (*Ask not what your country can do for you...*).
- **Foreshadowing:** Narrative clues planted early to anticipate future events.

### 7.2 Genres & Dramatic Conventions
- **Drama:** Direct imitation of human action designed for stage performance (*Greek origin: dran - to do/act*).
- **Comic Relief:** A humorous episode or character inserted in a tragedy to relieve emotional tension.
- **Hamartia:** Tragic flaw or error in judgment leading to the protagonist’s downfall.
- **Catharsis:** Purgation of the emotions of pity and terror.
- **Prose Classifications:** Broadly categorized into **Fiction** (novels, short stories, novellas) and **Non-fiction** (biography, autobiography, essays, memoirs).

### 7.3 Forms of Writing (Official Correspondence & Essays)
- **Formal Letters:** Require two addresses (sender top right, receiver top left), date, formal salutation, underlined title/heading, body, and complimentary close (*Yours faithfully* + signature + full name).
- **Memorandum (Memo):** Internal organizational communication with heading fields: *TO, FROM, DATE, SUBJECT*; no salutation or complimentary close required.
- **Minutes of Meetings:** Structured official record comprising: Attendance, apologies, reading/adoption of previous minutes, matters arising, substantive agenda items, A.O.B. (Any Other Business), adjournment, and signature spaces (Chairman & Secretary).
- **Paragraph Development:** Built around a **topic sentence**, developed through **unity**, **coherence** (linking devices), and **emphasis**.
