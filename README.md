# GES 201: Use of English II — CBT Master Quiz & Exam Simulator
### University of Ibadan • 2025/2026 Academic Session

A complete, high-performance, offline-friendly Computer-Based Test (CBT) training application and exam simulation system built specifically for **GES 201: Use of English II** students at the **University of Ibadan**.

The platform is designed around the exact structure, cognitive difficulty, command words, and distractor mechanics of the **GES 201 Central Test (LMS CBT)** and comprehensive degree examinations.

### Common Examiner Traps & Distractor Mechanics
1. **Phonetic Eye-Rhyme vs. Ear-Sound**:
   - The examiner presents words with identical spelling that have different vowel phonemes (e.g., *cough* `/ɒf/`, *rough* `/ʌf/`, *dough* `/əʊ/`, *plough* `/aʊ/`).
   - Words with silent consonants (e.g., *subtle* [silent 'b'], *indict* [silent 'c'], *corps* [silent 'p' & 's'], *rendezvous* [silent 'z']).
2. **Stress Suffix Exceptions & Shifts**:
   - Words shifting from second-syllable primary stress in the verb (`pro-DUCE`, `ex-PORT`) to first-syllable in the noun (`PRO-duce`, `EX-port`).
   - Suffixes that determine stress on the penultimate syllable (`-ic`, `-sion`, `-tion`) versus antepenultimate syllable (`-ity`, `-ate`, `-fy`, `-al`).
3. **Compound Noun vs. Phrasal Verb Stress**:
   - Phrasal verbs stress the particle/second element: *to fall OUT*, *to stand BY*.
   - Compound nouns stress the initial element: *a FALL-out*, *a STAND-by*.
4. **Poetic & Literary Interpretation Traps**:
   - Conflating *oxymoron* (two juxtaposed contradictory words: "cruel kindness") with *paradox* (a seemingly contradictory statement conveying deeper truth: "The Child is father of the Man").
   - Distinguishing Maurya's tragic resignation (*"They're all gone now, and there isn't anything more the sea can do to me..."*) from passive indifference or religious joy.

## 🛡️ Anti-Giveaway Exam Engine
To ensure authentic exam preparation, the system eliminates traditional test-taker guessing shortcuts:
1. **Dynamic Option Shuffling & Re-Lettering**:
   - In both Practice and Exam modes, options are randomized dynamically per question and assigned fresh `A, B, C, D` labels at render time.
   - Positional bias (e.g. "Option B is always correct") is completely destroyed.
2. **Substantive Distractor Balancing**:
   - Distractors across all 403 questions have been restructured to match the length, grammatical complexity, and academic tone of the correct answers.
   - Lone parenthetical explanatory notes in the correct answers have been eliminated or symmetrically balanced across all distractors.
   - Students cannot guess the answer by simply picking the longest or parenthesized option.

## 🛠️ How to Add, Edit, or Expand Questions

All questions reside in `data/questions.json`. Each entry strictly implements the following JSON schema:

```json
{
  "id": "GES201_SOUND_064",
  "category": "sound",
  "source": "textbook",
  "text_reference": "Chapter 2: English Speech Sounds, p. 54",
  "skill_tested": "vowel_phoneme_identification",
  "difficulty": "medium",
  "question": "Which of the following words contains the vowel sound /i:/ as in 'meat'?",
  "options": [
    "A. live",
    "B. police",
    "C. busy",
    "D. build"
  ],
  "correct_answer": "B. police",
  "explanation": "In 'police' (/pəˈliːs/), the second syllable has the long high front unrounded vowel /i:/. Options A, C, and D contain /ɪ/ ('live' /lɪv/, 'busy' /ˈbɪzi/, 'build' /bɪld/).",
  "confidence": "high"
}
```

### Field Definitions:
- **`id`** *(string)*: Unique identifier formatted as `GES201_{MODULE}_{3-DIGIT-INDEX}`.
- **`category`** *(string)*: Must be one of:
  - `"drama"` — J.M. Synge's *Riders to the Sea*
  - `"novel"` — Buchi Emecheta's *Second-Class Citizen*
  - `"poem"` — Prescribed 8 poems
  - `"sound"` — Phonetics, vowel/consonant charts, silent letters
  - `"stress"` — Syllable weight, suffix rules, stress shifts
  - `"intonation"` — Tone units, pitch contours, falling/rising tunes
  - `"literary_terms"` — Literary devices and dramatic conventions
  - `"writing_skills"` — Formal correspondence, reports, memoranda, punctuation
- **`source`** *(string)*: `"CA"` (Actual 2025/2026 Central Test question), `"textbook"` (Official UI course textbook), `"syllabus"` (Departmental course outline), or `"predicted"` (High-yield examination probability item).
- **`text_reference`** *(string)*: Exact chapter, page number, poem line, or scene reference.
- **`skill_tested`** *(string)*: Cognitive skill (e.g., `stress_placement_rule`, `silent_consonant`, `thematic_analysis`, `tragic_flaw`).
- **`difficulty`** *(string)*: `"easy"`, `"medium"`, or `"hard"`.
- **`question`** *(string)*: The prompt text. For stress questions, the target word is typically hyphenated with capitalized syllables (e.g., `con-tem-PO-ra-ry`).
- **`options`** *(array of strings)*: 4 or 5 options formatted with uppercase prefix letters (`"A. ..."`, `"B. ..."`, etc.).
- **`correct_answer`** *(string)*: Must exactly match one of the entries in `options`.
- **`explanation`** *(string)*: Pedagogical justification explaining why the correct choice is valid and pinpointing the exact distractor mechanics for other choices.
- **`confidence`** *(string)*: `"high"` or `"verified"`.

### Re-Assembling or Validating the Bank
You can add questions directly to `data/questions.json` or add them into the respective `bank_{module}.py` files and run:

```bash
python assemble_bank.py
```

The script will automatically validate ID uniqueness, verify that every `correct_answer` is present in `options`, and report category totals.
